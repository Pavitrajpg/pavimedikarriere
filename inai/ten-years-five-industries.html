Career

Ten years. Five industries.
One pattern.

Pavitra Muthukrishnan  ·  June 2025  ·  6 min read

People who have followed a linear career path within one industry sometimes look at a multi-industry background and see chaos. Different sectors, different job titles, no single vertical to point to. From the outside it can look like someone who never committed.

I used to feel a version of that discomfort myself. In interviews, the question of why I had moved across so many different fields always required a longer answer than the question seemed to invite.

What I understand now, having looked back across a decade of it, is that the career was not scattered. It was pattern-seeking. And the pattern it was looking for was the same in every industry it moved through.

The industries, and what each one taught.

Industry 1
Broadcast Media
Live television is unforgiving. There is no second take, no version two, no opportunity to fix it after it has gone out. Working in broadcast teaches a specific kind of discipline: you do the preparation, you trust the process, and then you execute. The pressure is real and the standards are public. Everything that followed was easier because of what broadcast established about what it means to be ready.
Industry 2
Healthcare Marketing
Healthcare is where I learned that the right message delivered the right way changes outcomes in ways that have nothing to do with the quality of the underlying product. We lifted campaign conversion from 20% to 55% not by changing what the client offered, but by changing how it was communicated and to whom. The product was the same. The communication was different. The result was different. That distinction has informed every piece of work since.
Industry 3
Luxury Retail
Tanishq was the industry that taught me the difference between selling and serving. In premium retail, you do not push product. You understand the person in front of you, you understand what they are actually trying to do, and you help them do it in a way that makes them feel understood rather than sold to. Achieving 120% of sales target came from relationship building, not from persuasion tactics. The lesson was that premium outcomes come from premium attention to the human being in the room.
Industry 4
Creative Production
Vibe Media was where operations and creativity met properly for the first time. Managing client relationships across jewellery, healthcare, and lifestyle accounts simultaneously meant holding multiple brand voices in your head at once and producing the right thing for the right client without letting them bleed into each other. The operational discipline of broadcast met the creative flexibility of agency work. Both were necessary. Neither alone was sufficient.
Industry 5
International Education and AI
Step4Progress was where everything converged. Building a CRM from scratch, designing a content strategy, counselling students through life-changing decisions, and integrating AI tools into operational workflows all at the same time. The full stack of what a decade of cross-industry experience actually meant in practice, applied to a single business problem at once. This was the moment the pattern became unmistakable.

The pattern that runs through all of it.

In every industry, in every role, the same thing was true. The organisations and campaigns and projects that produced results were the ones where the right things were connected to each other. The ones that did not produce results were almost always struggling with a disconnection somewhere in the chain.

A good product with no communication strategy. A strong team with no operational structure. A clear vision with no system to execute it. The problem was never the quality of the ingredient. The problem was the missing connection.

Broadcast taught connection between preparation and execution. Healthcare taught connection between message and audience. Retail taught connection between human need and brand response. Production taught connection between creative instinct and operational discipline. Education technology taught connection between all of the above simultaneously.

The industries were different. The lesson was the same.

What this means for the businesses INAI works with.

When a business brings a problem to INAI, the cross-industry background is not a liability. It is the asset. Because the most useful thing a strategist can bring to a problem is the ability to recognise it as a version of a problem they have seen before, in a different context, solved by a different method, that now applies here.

A study abroad consultancy with no lead tracking system is structurally the same problem as a healthcare campaign with a broken follow-up sequence. A brand that does not communicate what it actually does is the same problem as a luxury retail floor where the salesperson is talking about product features instead of listening to the customer. The context differs. The solution architecture has the same shape.

Ten years of working across five industries is not a winding path. It is a decade of learning how the same problem appears in different forms, and building the toolkit to solve each version of it.

The career that looked scattered from the outside was always following a single thread. The thread was connection. The work was always about finding what was not connected and connecting it. INAI is just what that work looks like when it has a name.

Pavitra Muthukrishnan, Founder, INAI
