Origin Story

Why I named it INAI.

Pavitra Muthukrishnan  ·  June 2025  ·  5 min read

Every morning in Tamil Nadu, before the day properly begins, women draw kolams at the threshold of their homes. They start with a grid of dots. Then, using rice flour or chalk, they draw a single continuous line that weaves between and around every dot until the pattern is complete.

No dot is left unconnected. No line is wasted. The whole thing is done from memory, in minutes, at the start of the day, every day. And then it is walked over, rained on, and swept away. The next morning, it begins again.

I grew up watching this. I thought it was decoration. It took me ten years of working across five industries to understand what it actually was.

The long way around.

My career did not follow a straight line. Broadcast media. Healthcare marketing. Luxury retail. Creative production. International education. From the outside, it looked like someone who could not decide what she wanted to be. From the inside, it felt like following something I could not yet name.

At Cauvery News, I learned what it meant to get things right the first time, under pressure, in public. At BCC Martech in healthcare, I learned that the right message delivered to the right person changes outcomes. At Tanishq, I learned that premium experiences are not sold, they are felt. At Vibe Media, I learned that the gap between a client's vision and their audience's experience is always a communication problem. At Step4Progress, I learned that a business without systems is a business that cannot scale, no matter how good the people inside it are.

Different industries. Different problems. But underneath all of it, the same pattern kept appearing.

The businesses that grew were the ones where the right things were connected to each other. Ideas to systems. People to opportunities. Strategy to execution.

The ones that struggled were the ones where those connections were missing. A good product with no communication strategy. A strong team with no operational structure. A clear vision with no digital presence to carry it.

Every intervention that worked came down to making a connection that was not there before.

The word that already existed.

When I decided to build something of my own, I did not start by trying to name it. I started by trying to describe what it did. I wrote it out in different ways across several weeks.

Connecting creativity and technology. Bridging ideas and outcomes. Linking people, systems, and growth.

And then one evening I looked at what I had written and realised there was already a Tamil word for all of it.

INAI. இணை.

To connect. To unite. To bring together.

It is not a metaphor. It is literally the thing. The Tamil word for what every successful outcome I had ever been part of had in common.

The kolam was always the answer.

Once I had the name, I went back to the kolam. And I understood it differently.

The kolam is not decoration. It is a daily practice of connection. Every dot represents something: a person, an idea, a system, a moment. The line that connects them is the work. The pattern that results is the outcome. And it is created fresh every day because connection is not something you do once. It is something you do again and again, with intention, until the pattern becomes second nature.

That is what INAI is built around. Not a service offering. Not a category. A philosophy of how growth actually happens.

Growth is not accidental. It is designed through meaningful connections between ideas, people, systems, and purpose.

The kolam at the centre of the INAI brand is not decorative. It is descriptive. It is the work.

What this means in practice.

When a business comes to INAI, we do not start by asking which service they need. We start by asking what is not connected. What is the gap between where they are and where they want to be?

Sometimes the gap is creative: they have a good product but no brand that communicates it clearly. Sometimes it is operational: they have a growing team but no systems to support the growth. Sometimes it is digital: they have something to say but nowhere that says it well. Sometimes it is strategic: they have budget and intent but no direction for either.

The pillar they need is INAI Creative, INAI Systems, INAI Digital, or INAI Growth. But the underlying work is always the same. Identify the missing connection. Make it. And build the pattern from there.

The kolam at my threshold is drawn fresh every morning. INAI was built the same way. Not as a finished thing, but as a living practice of connecting what needs to be connected.

If you have read this far, you probably have something that needs connecting too. I would like to hear what it is.

Pavitra Muthukrishnan, Founder, INAI
